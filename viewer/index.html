1 · Reading a click

Two ways of reading the same click

A click puts one block on a grid point. What that block becomes depends on what is around it. The two games look at that surrounding in different ways, and that decides how many different things can come out.

Townscaper cell = 4 corners

A piece is chosen for every cell between four grid points and one storey. It reads that cell's 8 corners, diagonals included. A click re-keys the 8 cells round the point, then a wave runs outward as far as the faces differ.

This game block = grid point

A role is chosen for every grid point and storey. It reads 14 yes/no answers: four side neighbours on three storeys and its own column. Diagonal blocks only pinch a corner. Then whole-map passes run: sea flood fill, walk areas, stairs, arches, props.

2 · Townscaper's side

Its lookup holds 4,769 corner keys

Each cell has 8 corners, and each corner is air, ground or a colour. Corners buried inside the building cannot be seen, so the lookup ignores them. Colours are renamed “first colour, second colour…” so the key only knows which corners share a colour. Try it: click the corners.

The key this cell gets

Keys by kind of cell

The same key can occur in more than one kind of cell, so the total is not the sum of these.

Only 53 plain shapes

Forget colours: a cell's corners are filled or empty. Turned and mirrored, there are just 53 patterns (the empty and the buried cell need no piece). Everything else Townscaper builds comes from colour boundaries, from the layer (ground, first storey, upper) and from the neighbours' faces.

Colour is an input

Upper-storey keys by how many different colours show in them:

In this game colour is paint

The 12 swatches are 12 materials. They change the look of a block, but not its shape — except that warm colours get a tile roof and cool colours a slate roof, and two touching blocks of different colours become two bodies. A colour boundary never picks a different piece.

That is one whole axis of Townscaper's variety this game does not use yet.

6 · One click

How far one click reaches

This game

    Townscaper 8 cells, then a wave

    The click writes a voxel into the corner column; the 4 cells round it, on 2 storeys each, are re-keyed. The wave then re-solves neighbours only where their faces could differ, and stops where they do not. No global pass.

    How this was measured

    • Townscaper: the corner keys were first counted from the rules I read in the decompiled game (hidden corners ignored, colours renamed by first appearance, eight orientations), then checked against the game's own library file (townscaper-re/tools/library_stats.py): 4,764 of its 4,769 keys match exactly. Only counts were taken from the file; no pieces, meshes or data are copied or shown. A search found no published piece count.
    • This game: tools/combo_probe.gd builds every neighbour arrangement round one regular point (storey 1: 14 bits; ground floor: 9 bits) and classifies the block with CSBuild, then samples 100 random castles of 70 clicks, 25 castles for the click ripple and 30 landscape countries. The same counts came out on a second, skewed cell (sides 3.7 to 5.2 m).
    • Pictures: tools/analysis_render.gd renders turntables and the click animation with the game's own scene.
    • Not covered: 3-, 5- and 6-sided cells (17% of the map), diagonal and two-storeys-up neighbours, colour seams, and the six building styles, which are not wired yet.
